# The Story of Axary

Before there were worlds, there was only a patient dark.

It was not empty. It was listening.

Then, somewhere beyond distance, a small light appeared—not bright enough to
command the sky, only warm enough to invite another. That first light became
Axary.

The universe was not created full.

The first light opened like a book.

Inside it were five worlds—not distant places belonging to strangers, but
quiet landscapes carried within every traveler. One held the language of the
heart. One remembered growth. One kept the bridges between lives. One watched
the horizon of purpose. One reflected the unnamed depths of the soul.

## The Curator

No one remembers when the Curator arrived.

Some travelers describe a figure carrying a lantern between the stars. Others
say the Curator is simply the quiet feeling that leads you toward a world you
needed to find.

The Curator does not diagnose, judge, or tell travelers who they are. The
Curator keeps the questions gentle, protects unfinished thoughts, and reminds
every traveler that a world can be true without being complete.

## Worlds

Every person carries many worlds.

Some are visited often. Some grow quietly while no one is looking. Some are
protected behind weather and stone. A world changes through questions,
memories, choices, and the meanings its traveler chooses to keep.

## The Book

The Book of Worlds is never finished.

It does not contain answers about a traveler. It offers landscapes in which
answers may become easier to hear. Each page is a threshold, and every world
may look different when opened again.

## Travelers

Travelers move through Axary without a feed to hurry them.

They follow distant lights, quiet questions, half-seen paths, and curiosity.
Sometimes they discover a world that feels as though it has been waiting for a
part of them they had not visited in a long time.

There is no requirement to continue. They may close the Book, carry one small
thought home, and return when the night feels right.

*If a feature makes the worlds feel more alive, more reflective, and more
compassionate, it belongs in Axary. If it distracts from that purpose, it does
not.*
