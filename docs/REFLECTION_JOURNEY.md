# The Reflection Journey

## Purpose

The Reflection Journey listens for patterns in how a visitor restores energy,
chooses, connects, creates, handles tension, meets change, finds meaning, and
approaches the unknown. Its output is environmental language—not a personality
type.

Axary does not claim that this journey measures a stable psychological trait or
offers clinical insight. It is an original reflective experience designed to
make symbolic worlds feel considered rather than random.

## Research Foundation

The design was informed by broad frameworks without copying their inventories:

- The Five-Factor Model demonstrates the value of thinking in continuous,
  overlapping dimensions instead of rigid categories. Axary borrows that
  dimensional principle, not its trait labels or test items.
- Schwartz's values research treats values as related motivational priorities.
  Axary uses balanced value choices to understand what a visitor wants their
  worlds to make visible, without ranking one visitor against another.
- Jung's symbolic work inspired open-ended imagery, personal meaning, and the
  idea that a symbol should not be reduced to one prescribed definition.
- Reflective coaching practice informed questions that preserve autonomy,
  invite awareness, and avoid telling the visitor what their answer means.

## Question Standards

Every question must:

- present psychologically comfortable, non-judgmental language;
- avoid an obviously desirable answer;
- distinguish between equally human ways of experiencing a situation;
- ask about a context or lived tendency instead of demanding a self-label;
- work without a diagnosis, score, or definitive interpretation;
- make optional writing genuinely optional;
- remain useful when combined with many different answer patterns.

## Interpretation Model

Responses contribute softly to multiple internal dimensions. A choice can
influence stillness and continuity at once; another can influence connection,
expression, and harmony. Ranked answers receive gradually decreasing weight,
while multi-select answers preserve several simultaneous priorities.

The resulting pattern creates:

- a versioned, deterministic private fingerprint;
- subtle resonance for Heart, Growth, Bonds, Purpose, and Soul;
- candidate environmental language for architecture, atmosphere, color,
  landmarks, light, music, vegetation, weather, and symbolism;
- open questions for future Curator conversations.

The fingerprint is not an identity, score, or public identifier. It simply
allows a particular answer pattern to produce a stable symbolic interpretation.

## Diversity Without Randomness

World variety comes from overlapping dimensions and attribute combinations,
not decorative randomness. Two visitors may both value belonging while
expressing different needs for privacy, continuity, openness, or directness.
Their Heart and Bonds worlds can therefore share a theme while differing in
paths, shelter, light, weather, and sound.

Small differences should be visible but proportionate. Axary must not turn one
answer into a dramatic environmental judgment.

## Privacy Boundary

Reflection answers belong to the visitor. They may personalize the visitor's
worlds and support future memories only with clear consent. They must never be
used for advertising, engagement targeting, comparison, ranking, eligibility,
or psychological diagnosis.

*If a feature makes the worlds feel more alive, more reflective, and more
compassionate, it belongs in Axary. If it distracts from that purpose, it does
not.*
